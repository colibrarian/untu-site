Chapter 4 · Persuasion · What happened

Eight tricks in two minutes

  1. Bandwagon: “everyone agrees”, but only friends were asked
    Every single person I talked to this week agrees with me.
  2. Appeal to emotion: fear
    Last Saturday a window was broken at our youth centre. Next time it could be a child who gets hurt. Can you live with that?
  3. Personal experience: one case
    My own nephew went to the centre on Saturdays for a year, and his grades went down. So we know what weekends there do to young people.
  4. Appeal to authority outside their field
    A famous football coach said on TV last month that young people need structure, not free time. If he says it, it is true.
  5. Convenient number, no source (anchoring)
    Some people say the centre costs only €2,000 a year to stay open on weekends. Others say €20,000. Let us be realistic and say it is closer to €20,000.
  6. False dilemma: only two options
    There are only two options. Either we close the centre on weekends, or we accept vandalism in our town.
  7. Hasty generalisation: one example, no comparison
    The town next to ours closed its centre on weekends five years ago, and they have had no problems since.
  8. Appeal to tradition
    And remember: our grandparents grew up without youth centres at all, and they turned out fine.

What is left of the speech without them?
