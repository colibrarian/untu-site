Objectives

What the training course sets out to build, in five parts.

01

Critical Thinking & Media Literacy

Practical, transferable tools for working with critical thinking and media literacy in youth work.

02

Dialogue & Public Speaking

Stronger ability to run structured discussions, debates and public‑speaking activities with young people.

03

Podcasting

Practical skills in podcast design, scripting, recording and basic editing as a tool for digital youth work.

04

Misinformation & Biases

Working with misinformation, cognitive biases, manipulation and polarisation.

05

Digital Youth Work Innovation

Responsible content creation, ethical communication and modern digital methods.

What Will We Actually Do?

Seven training days, one shared thread: turning critical thinking into real communication.

Full programme content +
  • Critical thinking and structured reasoning
  • Cognitive biases and logical fallacies
  • Misinformation, source evaluation and digital manipulation
  • Media narratives and responsible digital participation
  • Structured debates and moderated dialogue
  • Public speaking, message clarity, argumentation and confidence
  • Practical podcast design and concept development
  • Scripting, interviewing and moderation
  • Recording and basic editing
  • Teamwork and peer feedback
  • Daily reflection
  • Youthpass competence mapping
  • Planning local follow‑up activities
Main Project Output
At least 5 podcast episodes

Minimum 30 minutes each. You are not only coming to learn — you will co‑create a real project result.

🎙 5+ EPISODES · 30+ MIN

Expected Learning Outcomes

The goal isn't "learning to record a podcast" — it's becoming a stronger youth worker / facilitator who can translate critical thinking into real communication.

Knowledge
  • Critical thinking, cognitive biases, and media manipulation mechanisms
  • Ethical frameworks for communication and digital content creation
  • Podcast production structure and narrative storytelling
  • Youthpass competence and self‑assessment frameworks
Skills
  • Facilitating critical‑thinking workshops, debates, and youth‑led discussions
  • Evaluating media content, verifying sources, and deconstructing biases
  • Delivering structured, persuasive public messages
  • End‑to‑end podcast production (scripting, interviewing, recording, and editing) as a youth‑work tool
Attitudes
  • Commitment to ethical, inclusive, and fact‑based communication
  • Openness to diverse perspectives and continuous self‑reflection
  • Confidence in facilitating dialogue and adopting digital youth‑work practices
Youthpass

All participants will have the opportunity to receive a Youthpass certificate. It's not just a certificate of attendance — throughout the week you'll regularly reflect on what you've learned and map the development of your own competences, built directly into the project's methodology.

Who Should Apply

Participant Profile

  • Youth workers, trainers, educators and facilitators
  • People actively involved in non‑formal education, NGOs, youth centres, community organisations or youth initiatives
  • Both emerging and experienced youth professionals
  • People working directly with young people
  • People interested in critical thinking, media literacy, communication and digital youth work
  • Youth workers supporting migrants, refugees, rural youth, multicultural communities or young people with fewer opportunities
  • A limited number of youth‑oriented content creators / bloggers / vloggers / digital storytellers, if their work is directly connected to youth engagement or educational / community activities
Selection Qualities +

We are looking for participants who:

  • are motivated to actively participate during the whole programme;
  • can communicate in working English;
  • are willing to contribute to podcast production;
  • are ready to share experience from their own youth‑work context;
  • are committed to implementing and disseminating the learning after returning home.

Your Participation Doesn't End With the Training Course

"From Thinking to Speaking" is designed as a learning and multiplier project. By joining the Training Course, participants agree to take an active role not only during the mobility in Latvia, but also in transferring the results afterwards.

Each participant must actively contribute to the implementation of a local follow‑up activity. Participants from the same country may organise the activity together. Each national team is expected to organise at least one follow‑up activity reaching at least 16 people.
🎯 Through the combined follow‑up activities of all participating countries, the project aims to reach at least 80 young people beyond the international training group.
Participant Commitments & Expected Results +

During and after the project, participants are expected to:

  • actively participate in the full 7‑day Training Course;
  • contribute to the creation of at least 5 podcast episodes of minimum 30 minutes each, together with the international group;
  • participate in daily reflection, self‑assessment and the Youthpass learning process;
  • together with their national team, organise at least one local follow‑up activity reaching at least 16 people after returning home;
  • provide basic evidence of the follow‑up activity, such as photos/videos, a short agenda, participant numbers and a short report;
  • help disseminate the podcasts and other project results through their organisation, youth work networks and/or relevant communication channels;
  • present or share the acquired methods within their local organisation/community;
  • support the practical use of at least one project methodology in their organisation's regular youth work;
  • participate in a short post‑project follow‑up/evaluation process;
  • prepare a simple personal/team follow‑up commitment plan before leaving Latvia.

We are looking for participants who are motivated not only to learn, but also to apply, share and multiply the results after the mobility.

Before You Travel

Insurance, packing, preparation & sustainability — the practical bits.

Insurance & Health +

Participants are responsible for having valid health insurance for the full travel and mobility period. EU/EEA participants can use their EHIC, but note that an EHIC does not replace full travel insurance in every situation.

Before the mobility we will collect: allergies, dietary requirements, relevant medical / support needs, accessibility needs, and an emergency contact.

What to Bring +

Essential: ID / passport, health insurance / EHIC, personal medication, comfortable indoor & outdoor clothes, slippers, toiletries, chargers.

For the training: laptop if available, smartphone, headphones / earphones, charger or power bank, notebook if preferred, and — if you have one — a lapel mic or other compact microphone.

Green touch: a reusable water bottle — no need to bring professional microphones, the project provides the core podcast equipment.

Intercultural Evening +

During the training, we will have an Intercultural Evening, where each national team will have the opportunity to present their country in a fun and informal way.

Please prepare something together with your national team in advance. You are welcome to bring:

  • traditional snacks or drinks;
  • music, stories, folklore or traditions;
  • a short presentation, quiz, game or other creative activity.

The idea is not to make a formal presentation, but to share something authentic about your country and culture with the international group.

Please plan this before travelling and bring any small items or food you would like to use during the evening.

Preparation Before Arrival +

Before the mobility, participants will be invited to an online preparation meeting introducing the project, trainers and learning framework, added to a shared communication group, and given a short preparatory self‑reflection task on critical thinking, media use and communication challenges in their own youth‑work context.

Sustainability During the Project +
  • Prefer green travel where assigned
  • Reusable bottles, minimise single‑use plastic
  • Waste separation
  • Digital rather than printed materials
  • Responsible consumption; local / seasonal products where possible
  • Shared / public transport when feasible

Getting to Vircava

Riga (RIX) → Jelgava → Vircava

Full travel guide — click to expand +

If you arrive by plane

Fly into Riga International Airport (RIX) — the closest major airport.

  • Airport → Riga centre: take bus No. 22 or the Airport Express shuttle to the Central Bus/Train Station (“Autoosta”), about 20–30 minutes. Buying an e‑ticket in advance (Narvesen kiosks or the Mobilly app) is recommended.
  • Riga → Jelgava by bus: board a Hansa Lines (or similar carrier) bus from Rīgas SAO — departs roughly every 20 minutes, ~55 minutes to Jelgava Bus Station, around €2.50.
  • Riga → Jelgava by train: suburban electric train from the Central Railway Station, every 30–60 minutes, ~45 minutes, approx. €2 one way, ticket at the station office or from the conductor.
  • Jelgava → Vircava by bus: route 6764 (Wanema) from Jelgava Bus Station, ~20 minutes, €1–2. Ask the driver to drop you near your accommodation.
  • Transfer option: an organised transfer direct from Jelgava to Vircava (~12 km, 15 min) is available — recommended for convenience or late arrivals.

Green travel (train, bus or car)

  • Bus & train: same route as above — reach Jelgava first, then bus 6764 to Vircava.
  • Driving: Vircava is ~55 km south‑west of Riga via the A8/E77 towards Jelgava, then local roads — about 49 minutes. Parking & public transport details will be covered in the online preparatory meeting.
  • International coaches typically stop at Riga Bus Station — continue by local bus or train from there.
Please aim to arrive in Jelgava on 8 November 2026, at 18:00 — training starts the next morning.
