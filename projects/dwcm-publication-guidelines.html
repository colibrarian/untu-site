Guidelines

Publication and dissemination guidelines.

A short guide for participants and partners on responsibly publishing and reposting project content.

1. Figures and statistics

  • Distinguish unique videos from views/impressions — these are not interchangeable metrics.
  • Always label estimates as estimates (e.g. "~" or "estimate"), not as precise, verified figures.
  • Do not sum participant numbers together with digital reach as if they were the same metric.

2. Credit and visual identity

  • When reposting content, always credit: "Erasmus+ KA153-YOU, co-funded by the European Union".
  • Tag partner organisation accounts: UNTU, International Volda, Rubikus e. V., Kambuka Kids MTÜ.
  • #UNTU_DWC #DailyWellnessChallenge

3. EU visibility

Where applicable and according to the project's publicity requirements, public material carrying the EU logo must include the full disclaimer text (see the "Co-funded by the European Union" section at the bottom of the project page). Download official EU logos ↗

🌱 Green Youth Work Practice

GREEN & SUSTAINABLE PRACTICES SUMMARY

This summary documents how environmentally responsible practices were integrated into the project's implementation, learning process and follow-up.

1. Sustainability approach

Sustainability was integrated into the Daily Wellness Challenge Movement as a practical part of everyday project implementation rather than a separate symbolic activity. The consortium balanced environmental responsibility with accessibility, inclusion and the practical needs of an international group.

2. Green practices during the training course

  • Digital-first learning and reduced printing — schedules, resources and documents were shared digitally; printing was kept only where a physical format added learning value.
  • Waste reduction and reusable materials — reusable materials were prioritised and waste was sorted at the venue.
  • Responsible food planning — emphasis on vegetarian and, where possible, local and seasonal food; quantities were carefully planned with minimal food waste.
  • Sustainable travel encouragement — participants were informed about and encouraged to use public and shared transport whenever realistic.
  • Digital project resources — the main outputs (the Mapping Report, two toolkits, Digital Awareness Campaign materials, the video library and the Online Learning Module) were published as open digital resources, supporting long-term reuse without repeated printing.

3. Sustainability in learning and youth-work practice

Environmental responsibility was also connected to the project's educational content. The Wellness Toolkit includes outdoor and nature-based learning methods — Silent Walk, Forest Bathing and scenario development — linking wellbeing with respectful use of natural environments. Participant-developed resources, including an inclusive one-day hike guide for refugee families, include concrete environmental principles such as using established paths, carrying out rubbish, and respecting wildlife and local communities.

Key learning: environmental sustainability in youth work is not dependent on one "green activity". It is built through repeated practical choices in travel, food, materials, resource use, outdoor behaviour and organisational planning.

4. What was adapted in practice

  • Travel choices depended on participants' geographical location and available connections — green travel was encouraged but not treated as a condition for participation.
  • Printing was reduced substantially, but selected printed and visual materials were still used when pedagogically useful.
  • The project focused on practical waste reduction, sorting and careful food planning rather than claiming a fully zero-waste activity.
  • Sustainable choices were balanced with inclusion, health, dietary needs, safety and accessibility.

5. Sustainability beyond the mobility & Green Youth Work Practice Repository

The environmental approach continues through the long-term use of project results and the transfer of methods into partner organisations. This summary serves as the project website's Green Youth Work Practice Repository — bringing together the practical sustainability measures used during the project and connecting them with reusable educational resources.

6. Conclusion

The Daily Wellness Challenge Movement demonstrated that environmentally responsible project implementation can be practical, proportionate and compatible with inclusion. The strongest contribution was not a single environmental action, but the integration of sustainable choices throughout the project — from how the training was organised to how results remain accessible after the mobility.
