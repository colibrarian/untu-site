Podcasting · Memo

On Air Together

How to talk to each other while recording a podcast, so that everyone is heard and the episode stays on time.

The short version

Before
Pick a moderator or a timekeeper. Agree on topics, time and hand signals.
During
One voice at a time. Understand first, then disagree. Argue with the idea, not the person. Speak for yourself. You can pass. Respect the shared time.
After
One thing that worked, one thing to change next time.
01

Before recording

5 minutes of agreements

Choose roles before you press Record.

Three people

Two people talk, the third one moderates. The moderator does not argue or give their own opinion. They lead the conversation.

Everyone talks

There is no moderator, but one person still takes the role of timekeeper: they hold the timer and the topic list, tick off what has been covered and say when it is time to move on.

Agree on the plan: which topics, roughly how many minutes for each, in what order, and how long the whole episode is.

Agree on silent signals. On air you cannot interrupt with your voice, so use your hands:

Raised handfrom the moderator: please finish your sentence
Raised fingerfrom a speaker: I want to say something
Palm downfrom anyone: let's pause the recording

Say the rules out loud at the start, so everyone hears the same thing.

02

The moderator's job

When one person leads

Main goal: everyone gets the chance to speak.

  • Watch the balance. Notice who has spoken a lot and who has spoken little. Invite quiet people by name.

    “Ana, how is it in Spain?”

  • Stop long answers gently.

    “Thank you, let's stop on this thought. I'd like to hear Lukas too.”

  • Stay neutral. Do not judge answers (“right”, “strange”) and do not take sides.
  • Sum up briefly before moving to the next topic.
  • Keep the time and the topic list.
  • If it gets tense, ask for a pause and go back to the rules. Do not look for who is to blame.
03

When everyone talks

No moderator
  • Everyone is responsible for the balance. If you notice someone has been quiet for a long time, invite them in.
  • Respect the shared time, even if you need more. The podcast belongs to everyone. One clear thought is better than three unfinished ones. If something important is left, say “I'll come back to this at the end”, and come back if there is time.
  • Everyone follows the timekeeper's signal, including the timekeeper. They are not the boss, they are the group's clock.
04

Rules of conversation

For every speaker
  1. One voice at a time. Listen until the end. Interrupting also ruins the recording: two voices at once cannot be edited.
  2. Understand first, then disagree. Before you disagree, briefly repeat what you heard.

    “If I understood you right, you are saying that…”

  3. Argue with the idea, not the person. No labels, no sarcasm, no “you always…”. Irony is easily misread in an international group.
  4. Speak for yourself: “I think”, “in my country”, not “all of them”.
  5. You can pass on any question, without explaining why.
  6. English is not the first language for most of us. Speak slowly and simply, ask again if you did not understand. Do not correct other people's English and do not joke about accents.
  7. Not only words. While someone else is speaking, do not roll your eyes, whisper or sit on your phone. Listeners may not hear it, but everyone in the room sees it.
  8. Disagreement is fine. For listeners it is the most interesting part. The goal is not to win but to show different views.
  9. Other people's stories only with their consent. Do not share personal things about other people or name them without permission.
05

When it goes wrong

Pause, rules, then on
  • Anyone can ask to pause the recording. It is not a failure, it is a normal part of the work.
  • During the pause the moderator or timekeeper reminds everyone of the rules and the time plan, without discussing who is to blame.
  • A personal conflict is not solved on air. Talk about it after the recording, one to one or with a trainer.
06

After recording

A 5-minute round

A short round where nobody rates anybody:

  • One thing that worked for us.
  • One thing we will do differently next time.
